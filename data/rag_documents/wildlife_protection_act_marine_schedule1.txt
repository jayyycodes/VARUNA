MINISTRY OF ENVIRONMENT, FOREST AND CLIMATE CHANGE
WILDLIFE (PROTECTION) ACT, 1972
SCHEDULE I — PROTECTED MARINE AND COASTAL FAUNA

Section 9: Prohibition of Hunting and Capture
No person shall hunt, capture, injure, kill, or trade any wild animal specified in Schedule I of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.

Protected Marine Fauna under Schedule I:
1. Marine Turtles:
- Olive Ridley Sea Turtle (Lepidochelys olivacea)
- Green Sea Turtle (Chelonia mydas)
- Hawksbill Turtle (Eretmochelys imbricata)
- Leatherback Turtle (Dermochelys coriacea)
Use of Turtle Excluder Devices (TED) in trawl nets is mandatory within 20 km of turtle congregation and mass-nesting beaches (especially in Odisha: Gahirmatha, Rushikulya; and Maharashtra: Velas, Anjarle).

2. Marine Mammals:
- Dugong / Sea Cow (Dugong dugon) — Protected habitat in Gulf of Mannar, Palk Bay, and Gulf of Kutch.
- Gangetic Dolphin (Platanista gangetica) and Irrawaddy Dolphin (Orcaella brevirostris).
- All species of Whales and Dolphins found in Indian waters.

3. Elasmobranchs and Fish:
- Whale Shark (Rhincodon typus) — First marine fish species to be placed under Schedule I in India (2001).
- Sawfishes (Pristidae family) and Giant Guitarfish.

4. Invertebrates and Corals:
- All species of hard corals (Scleractinia) and black corals (Antipatharia).
- Sea cucumbers (Holothuroidea) — Total ban on harvesting, possession, and commercial trade across all Indian maritime states.

Emergency Protocols for Bycatch:
If any Schedule I marine animal is inadvertently entangled or caught as bycatch in fishing gear:
1. The vessel master must immediately halt operations and carefully release the animal unharmed back into the ocean.
2. If the net must be cut to save the animal, the State Forest Department provides financial compensation for net damage upon verification.
3. The incident must be reported to the nearest Range Forest Officer or Fisheries Development Officer with date, GPS coordinates, and photographic proof.
Penalties: Violations carry mandatory imprisonment between 3 to 7 years and a minimum fine of Rs. 25,000.
